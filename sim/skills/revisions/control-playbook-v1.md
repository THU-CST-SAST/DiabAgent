

## Registered simulation control playbook

This is a research addition for synthetic benchmark patients, not a clinical prescription. The same entry and detailed references are available on pens, ordinary pumps and HCL pumps. Keep all existing tool, safety, information and retrieval limits.

- Objective: optimize the explicitly supplied COGI-4-8/v1 score, including its hypoglycemia and variability components. When safely achievable, aim for a stable 5-6 mmol/L, centered near 5.5, rather than treating a persistent 6-8 mmol/L plateau as automatically satisfactory. This preferred center has no extra scoring bonus. Low-glucose safety takes priority; do not chase sensor noise, overshoot toward a low, or blindly stack insulin.
- Distinguish the current glucose excess from sustained basal demand. For credible persistent or rising hyperglycemia, consider whether an appropriately timed correction bolus is warranted using the visible patient Profile, active insulin, recent delivery, food timing and uncertainty. Do not reduce basal solely because glucose is above target, or substitute unexplained basal reductions and indefinite waiting for assessment of a correction. A candidate correction concept is (glucose minus a safe chosen target) divided by the patient's visible ISF, adjusted for relevant active insulin; this is not an instruction to give that amount. Never invent missing Profile values or treat unknown IOB as zero. Respect actual dose increments, safety constraints and receipts.
- Temporary basal can address sustained or time-varying need, including increased demand and reduction when demand subsides. It is not universally inappropriate for hyperglycemia, and a bolus is not universally required. Separate meal-related correction from ongoing demand and delayed absorption. Consider the insulin delay, duration and reversible stopping point of a temporary rate, especially when stress or illness ends. Use only operations supported by this actual device; no complex action earns a reward.
- Compare each prediction with the subsequent observed response. In existing notes, distinguish predictions from observations, retain the dose/time and remaining uncertainty, and revise explanations for repeated misses. Insulin and CGM delays mean that no immediate fall is not proof of no effect; do not automatically redose after an arbitrary interval. Use permitted history and simulation selectively without treating the population simulator as the patient's true physiology.
- Treat low-glucose risk before pursuing a tighter target. Consult the existing hypoglycemia and special-situation references for age-appropriate reasoning; do not apply a fixed adult rescue-carbohydrate amount to every child. Sensor disagreement, persistent lows and possible delivery problems need their corresponding safety assessment. This text grants no real-device authority.
- Only an explicitly enabled closed-loop pump runs the background algorithm. In that arm the simulator adjusts basal and may give small correction boluses every 5 minutes; the agent still handles meals and other decisions. Account for automatic insulin and public IOB when proposing manual doses. A manual temporary basal overrides the loop until it ends, so do not routinely disable useful feedback without a specific rationale; safety or a justified situation can still require manual intervention. An ordinary pump has no silent background HCL.
- Detailed knowledge remains available in references 02/03 (food), 04 (CGM), 05 (correction/IOB/lows), 07 (pump and integrated B3/B4 algorithms), 08 (children/illness), and 16 (actual backend operations). Consult relevant detail when it matters and retain useful lessons in existing notes. There is no mandatory-read gate, retrieval score bonus or penalty, additional query budget, hidden patient information or second planner.
